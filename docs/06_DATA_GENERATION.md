## 1. Strategy and provenance

### 1.1 Why synthetic

No public dataset provides store-SKU-day sales **and** inventory balances **and** purchase orders **and** supplier receipts for a multi-store retailer; corporate data is confidential. stocgain therefore uses a **deterministic causal simulation** of a fictional 12-store home-improvement retailer.

### 1.2 Provenance statement (used verbatim in `dataset_manifest.notes`, README, and the Methodology page)

> **Simulated Retail Dataset.** All stores, products, suppliers, prices, sales, inventory, and purchase orders in stocgain were generated by the stocgain simulator (version {generator_version}, preset {preset}, seed {seed}) for the period {start} to {end}. City names are real US metros; all store, product, brand, and supplier names are fictional, and any resemblance to real businesses is coincidental. The data is not derived from, and does not represent, any real company. Results describe the simulation and are not evidence about real retail operations.

### 1.3 Principles

1. **Causally coherent:** sales are caused by demand and constrained by stock; stock is caused by receipts, sales, and adjustments; receipts are caused by purchase orders placed by a replenishment policy; delays are caused by supplier behavior.
2. **Deterministic:** identical outputs for identical (preset, seed, generator version).
3. **Observable vs hidden:** latent demand is written only to `sim_truth.latent_demand`.
4. **Imperfect retailer:** the simulated retailer uses a deliberately **naive legacy replenishment policy** (orders based on raw recent sales, including stockout zeros). This produces realistic stockouts and the censored-demand problem stocgain is designed to detect.
5. **Compact facts:** daily store-SKU aggregates, not individual transactions.

### 3.5 Lanes

For each supplier × store in the preset:

```
contract_lead_time_days = supplier_base_LT + region_LT_offset + (1 with probability 0.3 else 0)   # ≥ 1
review_weekday = 1 + (hash(supplier_id, store_id, seed) mod 5)                                   # Mon–Fri
```

`hash` is a deterministic integer hash (e.g., from the lanes RNG stream); never Python's salted `hash()`.

### 3.6 Calendar

Rows for every date from `history_start − burn_in_days` through `history_end + 90`. ISO fields from Python `date.isocalendar()`. Holidays (2026): Memorial Day 2026-05-25, Independence Day 2026-07-04, Labor Day 2026-09-07 (plus their 2025 equivalents for the large preset: Labor Day 2025-09-01, Thanksgiving 2025-11-27, Christmas 2025-12-25, New Year's Day 2026-01-01).

## 4. Random seed strategy (`generator/rng.py`)

- Use NumPy's `Generator` with `SeedSequence`. One independent stream per component:

```python
STREAMS = {"catalog": 1, "suppliers": 2, "lanes": 3, "promotions": 4,
           "demand": 5, "fulfillment": 6, "adjustments": 7, "policy": 8, "events": 9}
def stream(seed: int, name: str) -> np.random.Generator:
    return np.random.default_rng(np.random.SeedSequence([seed, STREAMS[name]]))
```

- Independent streams mean changing, e.g., the adjustment model does not shift demand draws.
- Never use the `random` module, Python `hash()`, wall-clock time, set iteration order, or unordered dict iteration in generation logic. Iterate positions in sorted `(store_id, product_id)` order.
- `generated_at` (timestamp) is the only non-deterministic field and is excluded from checksums.
- **Determinism test:** generating a preset twice yields byte-identical CSV files (SHA-256 per file and combined `content_checksum`).

## 5. Demand model (`generator/demand.py`)

### 5.1 Latent demand

For store `s` (region `r`), product `p` (category `c`), date `d`:

```
λ(s,p,d) = b_p                       product base rate (chain-average units/store/day)
         × v_s                       store volume factor (§3.1)
         × ρ(r,c)                    region–category affinity, lognormal(0, 0.08), fixed per (r,c)
         × season(c, r, d)           §3.2
         × dow(c, d)                 normalized weekday profile
         × hol(c, d)                 holiday factor (§5.3)
         × promo_true(p, r, d)       realized promotion lift (§5.4), 1 if none
         × (1 + g_p × (d − start_burn_in) / total_days)   product trend
         × event(s, p, d)            demo events (§8), 1 if none

latent(s,p,d) ~ NegativeBinomial(mean = λ, dispersion k_p)     variance = λ + λ²/k_p
```

`k_p ∈ {20 (stable), 5 (moderate), 1.5 (volatile)}` drawn per product from the category's dispersion mix. NumPy parameterization: `n = k`, `p = k / (k + λ)`. If `λ < 1e−6`, latent = 0.

### 5.2 Intermittency and slow movers

No special mechanism is needed: low `λ` with NB noise produces many zero days naturally (an intermittent product with `λ = 0.05` sells on ~5% of days).

### 5.3 Holidays

| Window | Categories | Factor |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Memorial Day weekend (Fri 2026-05-22 → Mon 2026-05-25) | LG, PA, BM, PT | × 1.30 |
| Labor Day weekend (Fri 2026-09-04 → Mon 2026-09-07) | LG, PA, BM, PT | × 1.25 |
| Independence Day (2026-07-04) | all | × 0.80 |

### 5.4 Promotions (`generator/promotions.py`)

- Generated from `history_start − burn_in_days` through `history_end + 42` (future promotions are **plans**: they appear in `ops.promotions` but have no realized sales).
- Cadence: for each category, a new promotion starts every 12–18 days (uniform integer), duration 7–14 days.
- Scope: chain-wide with probability 0.4, else one random region.
- Products: 4–8 products from the category (small preset: 2–3), excluding intermittent-tier products, and excluding products already in an overlapping promotion with overlapping scope (resample; DQ09 must pass).
- `discount_rate ~ U(0.10, 0.30)` rounded to 0.05 steps.
- Category elasticity `e_c ~ U(1.5, 3.0)` (fixed per category).
- `planned_lift = round(1 + e_c × discount_rate, 2)`, clipped to [1.05, 2.50].
- Realized lift per promotion: `promo_true = planned_lift × U(0.85, 1.15)` (same for all its days and stores). Planners are roughly right, not exact.
- Names: "{Season word} {Category} {Event word}" (e.g., "Spring Lawn & Garden Event", "Fall Plumbing Repair Event", "Summer Paint Savings").
- `promotion_code = 'PRM-' || lpad(promotion_id, 4, '0')`.
- Price on promo days: `unit_price × (1 − discount_rate)` for `net_sales_amount`.

## 6. Inventory and replenishment simulation (`generator/simulation.py`)

### 6.1 State per position (NumPy arrays of length N = stores × products)

`on_hand`, `open_orders` (scheduled shipments keyed by arrival date → list of `(po_line_id, position_index, qty)`), `sales_history` (rolling 28-day buffer of observed sales for the legacy policy).

### 6.2 Initialization (first burn-in day)

```
expected_daily = b_p × v_s × ρ(r,c) × season(c,r,day0)
on_hand_0 = ceil(expected_daily × (contract_LT + 7) × 1.2) + presentation_min_p
presentation_min_p = 2 if unit_price > 100 else case_pack_qty
```

No open POs at initialization. The 35-day burn-in lets ordering, receipts, and stockouts reach a realistic steady state before the recorded window.

### 6.3 Daily loop (for each date from burn-in start through `history_end`)

Event order is binding (matches 03 §5.1 and 05 §1):

```
1. RECEIPTS (start of day)
   for each scheduled shipment arriving today: on_hand[pos] += qty; write inventory_receipts row
   opening_on_hand = on_hand before receipts; available = opening + received_today

2. DEMAND AND SALES
   latent = NB(λ(s,p,d), k_p)
   sold = min(latent, available)
   net_sales = sold × price × (1 − discount if promo); cogs = sold × cost
   on_hand = available − sold

3. ADJUSTMENTS (end of day), independently per reason with probabilities per position-day:
   shrink                 p = 0.0040, delta = −U{1..3}
   damage                 p = 0.0010, delta = −U{1..2}
   cycle_count_correction p = 0.0020, delta = ±U{1..3} (sign 50/50)
   vendor_return          p = 0.0003, delta = −U{1..4}
   Negative deltas are capped so on_hand never goes below 0; if the capped delta is 0, no row is written.
   closing_on_hand = on_hand

4. ORDERING (end of day; legacy policy, §6.4) for lanes whose review_weekday == ISO weekday(d)

5. RECORD (only for dates within [history_start, history_end]):
   daily_sales, inventory_snapshots (opening, closing), latent_demand, adjustments
   (POs, PO lines, and receipts are recorded for all dates including burn-in)
```

### 6.4 Legacy replenishment policy (the simulated retailer's own process)

Intentionally naive, documented as such on the Methodology page ("how the simulated retailer orders today"):

```
for each lane (supplier, store) reviewed today, for each product whose primary supplier is that supplier:
    avg = mean(observed units sold over the last 28 days)          # includes stockout zeros — the flaw
    IP  = closing_on_hand + Σ open order quantity for this position
    s_legacy = ceil(avg × contract_LT × β_p) + presentation_min_p
    S_legacy = ceil(avg × (contract_LT + 7) × β_p) + presentation_min_p
    if IP ≤ s_legacy:
        qty = max(MOQ, ceil((S_legacy − IP) / case_pack) × case_pack)
        add PO line
if the lane has ≥ 1 line today: create one PO (order_date = d, promised = d + contract_LT)
```

Consequences (desired): products that stock out show lower `avg`, get smaller orders, and stock out again until the presentation minimum and stochastic demand break the cycle — the censored-demand death spiral. Products with low `β_p` are chronically tight.

### 6.5 Supplier fulfillment

At PO creation, for each PO, draw (fulfillment stream):

```
u ~ U(0,1)
if u < 0.08:               delay = −1                              (early)
elif u < 0.08 + p_s:       delay = 0                               (on time)
else:                      delay = 1 + Poisson(λ_s)                (late)
delay += event_delay(supplier, order_date)                          (§8)
arrival = max(order_date + 1, promised + delay)
```

All lines of a PO arrive together on `arrival` (first shipment). Per line, the fill behavior (§3.3) determines the quantity in the first shipment, any second shipment (3–7 days later), or a cancellation (`quantity_cancelled` set on the line).

Arrivals after `history_end` are **not written** (the POs remain open in the database). The generator must never write a receipt dated after `history_end`.

### 6.6 PO numbering

`purchase_order_id` sequential from 100001 in creation order; `po_number = 'PO-' || purchase_order_id`. `po_line_id` sequential from 300001; `receipt_id` from 500001; `adjustment_id` from 70001.

| E3 | Chronically unreliable plumbing supplier | `demo_pl_unreliable_supplier` (SUP-021) uses `p_s = 0.70`, `λ_s = 3.5` for the whole period | Lowest 90-day on-time rate among Plumbing suppliers; `supplier_unreliable` and `lead_time_variability` drivers; Review flag |
| E4 | End of lawn season | Built into LG seasonality (no event code) | LG demand declining; forecast limitation visible in backtest bias |
| E5 | Planned fall promotions | At least one chain-wide Paint and one Plumbing promotion starting within 2026-10-01 → 2026-10-14 (forced by promotion generator when events enabled) | `upcoming_promotion` drivers; forecast lift in projection chart |

Setting `[events] enabled = false` produces a "neutral" dataset (useful for tests that must not depend on story effects).

## 10. Calibration targets (plausibility, not hard outcomes)

After generating the standard preset, `stocgain data profile` prints these statistics. They are **plausibility ranges** used to sanity-check the simulation; Codex may tune generator parameters within documented ranges to land inside them, and must record any tuning in `docs/DECISIONS_LOG.md`. They must never be met by editing outputs directly.

| Statistic | Target range |
|-----------|--------------|
| Chain share of `out_all_day` position-days | 2%–8% |
| Chain share of `sold_out` position-days | 1%–5% |
| End-of-day in-stock rate | 88%–97% |

| Mean PO lead time | 7–11 days |
